Kalpana Chawla

Kalpana Chawla was an Indian-born American astronaut and aerospace engineer. She was the first woman of Indian origin to go to space.

Early life and education

Kalpana Chawla was born on 17 March 1962 in Karnal, a town in what was then the state of Punjab and is now Haryana, India. She studied at Tagore Bal Niketan school in Karnal and earned a bachelor's degree in aeronautical engineering from Punjab Engineering College in Chandigarh in 1982.

She moved to the United States, earned a master's degree in aerospace engineering from the University of Texas at Arlington in 1984, and a doctorate in aerospace engineering from the University of Colorado Boulder in 1988.

NASA career

Chawla began working at NASA's Ames Research Center in 1988 and became a United States citizen in 1991. She was selected as an astronaut in 1994. Her first flight was on Space Shuttle Columbia as part of mission STS-87 in 1997, where she was a mission specialist and the primary robotic arm operator.

Death

Chawla died on 1 February 2003, along with the six other crew members, when Space Shuttle Columbia broke apart during re-entry at the end of mission STS-107.

Legacy

Schools, scholarships, a hostel at Punjab Engineering College and a medical college in Karnal are named after her. India's weather satellite MetSat-1 was renamed Kalpana-1 in her honour.
